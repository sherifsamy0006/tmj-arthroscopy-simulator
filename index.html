    MODEL & EVIDENCE

    One physical lens. Explicit reconstruction limits.

    r59 · navigation and orientation

    The lens image is steered by dragging, moved in and out with the wheel and rotated with a right-drag or Ctrl-drag. The map orbits with a drag, zooms toward the pointer with the wheel and pans with a right-drag; Ctrl-drag on the map turns the telescope about its own axis.

    The lens now stays inside the joint space it is in. Steering that would push the tip through the soft lining draws the telescope back along its own axis, and it eases forward again to the depth last chosen once there is room; it is never pushed deeper than the user advanced it. Where that is not enough, steering stops at the edge of the space. When bone or the disc stops a steering gesture, the telescope is eased back a little and the turn is tried again, so it slides along the surface instead of sticking. Scrolling back pauses once at the capsule entry before the lens leaves the joint; withdrawal is otherwise never restricted.

    These limits, the structure names on the map and the recess / zone readout (thirds of the model space) are illustrative teaching aids. They are not measured tissue mechanics, a force model or validated landmarks.

    r58 · procedure and appearance

    The guided procedure follows single-puncture arthroscopy: mandibular protrusion, insufflation, puncture and insertion, lavage through an outflow needle, then suction. Protrusion and insufflation each change the real joint geometry through one contact-checked rebuild; the map animates the requested movement while the check runs.

    Bleeding, blood clearance, suspended debris, bubbles, suction collapse and the lens optics (barrel distortion, near-field defocus, glare, field stop) are illustrative. Blood is a well-mixed level with drifting sprites, not a flow simulation; clearance times are chosen to be readable, not measured. The syringe, outflow needle and irrigation lines are teaching overlays without collision or a registered skin portal. Outside the joint spaces the lens shows a featureless soft-tissue blur.

    What the two views share

    The actual telescope tip, 30° optical direction and 65° field drive the arthroscopic image, blue locator cone and gold centre-ray marker. Tissue identification uses five rays through that same camera. Layer separation and cutaway affect the locator only.

    The cavity is rebuilt against the original bone and disc surfaces. An artificial internal bridge from the old reconstruction has been removed; soft attachments follow their corresponding source surfaces. The estimated posterior lining is rebuilt with a bounded enclosure with short broad irregular pleats, tapered away from original attachment surfaces at each jaw or pressure state. Exact source bone and disc surfaces are then subtracted. This removes repeated artificial attachment teeth and changes the estimated prepared upper-space volume by about 1.2%; it does not alter the original bones or disc. The resulting boundary supplies rendering, sections, location classification and fluid volume together.

    Movement & irrigation

    Ordered pointer samples preserve curved and combined movements independently of render timing. The external hand frame stays fixed when the telescope rotates. Obstructed steering preserves separately checked withdrawal and optical roll; rotation across ±180° takes the short path. The prepared lens starts 1 mm shallower than r52, providing more steering room within the unchanged hard surfaces. The full 1.9 mm × 63.1 mm shaft and conservative proximal envelope retain swept bone/disc collision checks. Rejected instrument requests stop at the last admitted position; withdrawal and optical roll remain available.

    Bone and disc are rigid in the prescribed disc–condyle cluster. This is a contact-constrained teaching approximation, not a natural disc trajectory. The inferior space co-moves without distension. Only the estimated superior lining receives the scalar pressure mode.

    What remains unmeasured

    Nexus 501200: 1.9 mm shaft, 63.1 mm working length, 115 mm overall, 30° direction and 65° field. Proximal shape is illustrated from manufacturer photographs. The 501201/501204 “1.9 mm / 50 mm” labels do not establish cannula outer diameter, lumen, seating datum or lens offset.

    The shaft pivot is fixed at the prepared model capsule entry. The purple ring shows the current model capsule crossing, not a registered skin portal. The posterior fold has been reconstructed, but tissue morphology and its extent are still estimated; visual realism is incomplete. Actual synovial geometry, specimen motion, cannula assembly and clinical realism still require suitable measurements and review. Prescribed motion and scalar lining compliance have model limits; software verification does not establish clinical realism.

    Contact checks, mechanics and numerical scope

    Source checksums pending.

    Hard bone/disc motion retains continuous distance bounds, including a conservative bound for rotation between admitted steps. The complete instrument is checked against moving tissues. At each step midpoint and endpoint, the rebuilt cavity is checked for closed, consistently wound topology, nonzero triangles and self-intersections beyond shared mesh boundaries. This soft-boundary check is sampled, not continuous detection. No pre-existing cavity penetration is allowed as a baseline. A 0.00002 mm extent/penetration tolerance handles Float32 clipping slivers; coplanar overlap checks remain active. Each pressure domain uses its principal connected component. Tiny excluded numerical pockets are recorded; a split above 0.001 of total volume or 0.00001 mL is rejected. Combined motion or distension stops when the model cannot supply an admissible shape.

    The pressure model has one scalar wall coordinate, uniform pressure and a well-mixed tracer. Its outward compliant mode tapers into fixed hard boundaries and contact anchors. At a model expansion limit the last admitted volume stays fixed while reduced inlet/outlet flow and tracer exchange continue. The model is not CFD, calibrated cannula resistance, tissue stress or clinical pressure prediction. Anterior/posterior volumes divide the actual upper cavity at model Y = 0; they do not simulate separate pressures.

    Microscopic fibres, vessels, wet-film reflection and exposure are illustrative. Their reference coordinates use the prepared joint to avoid stretching a collapsed neutral texture across the opened lining; they remain attached during motion. No clinical photograph is projected as the lens image. The same clinical pose cannot be registered to a photograph from a different specimen; comparisons assess qualitative morphology, not patient-specific correspondence.
